# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Throughline is a research-software project. We want technical discussion to be rigorous, specific, and useful while keeping participation respectful and safe.

Contributors, maintainers, reviewers, and users are expected to treat one another professionally regardless of experience level, background, identity, employer, institution, or viewpoint.

## Expected behavior

Examples of constructive participation include:

- criticizing ideas, code, methods, and evidence rather than people;
- explaining disagreements with reproducible examples or sources when possible;
- acknowledging uncertainty and correcting mistakes openly;
- giving actionable review feedback;
- respecting privacy, embargoed research, unpublished data, and confidential reports;
- assuming good faith while still holding security and scientific claims to a high standard;
- making space for contributors who are new to the codebase or domain.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable conduct includes:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained personal attacks;
- discriminatory or demeaning comments;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- deliberately exposing credentials, unpublished research, personal data, or security details entrusted privately;
- retaliating against someone for making a good-faith security, safety, scientific-integrity, or conduct report;
- repeatedly disrupting project spaces after maintainers ask for a behavior to stop.

Strong technical disagreement is not itself misconduct. Review comments may be direct when they remain about the work and provide enough context to be useful.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project-managed chat or meetings, and when someone is representing the project in an official capacity.

## Reporting

For ordinary conduct concerns, contact a maintainer privately rather than opening a public issue containing sensitive details. For security vulnerabilities, follow `SECURITY.md`.

Reports should contain enough context to understand what happened, including links or screenshots when safe to share. Remove credentials, personal data, private research material, and unrelated third-party information.

Maintainers will review reports in good faith and may request additional context. There is no guaranteed response SLA for this volunteer/early-stage project.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and history, maintainers may:

1. request a correction or change in behavior;
2. remove or edit disruptive content where platform controls permit;
3. restrict participation temporarily;
4. remove repository access or ban participation in project-managed spaces.

Maintainers with a conflict of interest should avoid making the final enforcement decision when another maintainer can reasonably handle it.

## Attribution

This policy is informed by common open-source community standards, including the Contributor Covenant, but is written specifically for Throughline's research and privacy context.
